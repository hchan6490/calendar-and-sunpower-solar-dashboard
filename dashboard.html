How to read this

Each dot is one full day: the kWh your panels produced divided by that day's “sun-hours” (hours of full-strength sun, from the weather data). Dividing out the sunshine means a cloudy day no longer looks like a problem — the number stays steady when the panels are healthy.

The line is a 14-day median. Dust usually shows up as a slow drift downward that rain (blue marks) partly reverses. A sudden one-day drop is more often shade, an offline panel, or bad data.

Heat makes panels less efficient, so “adjust for temperature” evens out summer vs. winter (it's a rough estimate). Even so, the cleanest comparison is the same time of year, which is what the “vs. last year” tile shows once there's a year of history.

The sunshine figures are modeled for your area rather than measured on your roof, so individual days are a bit noisy — trust the trend, not any single dot. Days with a logging gap, partial days, and very dim days are left out.
